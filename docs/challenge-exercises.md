# The Challenge Track: Your Path to an A / A+

This semester, our core textbook, labs, and class exercises are designed to ensure everyone—regardless of your major—can safely learn the fundamentals of programming and succeed in this class.

However, for students who want to push themselves, see what the "pros" use on a day-to-day basis, and earn the highest marks in the course (A or A+), we are introducing the Challenge Track.

The Challenge Track moves beyond the standard assignments and into a professional development environment using GitHub and VSCode.

## How It Works: The "Learning Journey" Portfolio

Instead of grading individual coding challenges with traditional points, your work will form a Trail of Evidence documenting your growth.

Grades in the A/A+ range are achieved via demonstrated engagement beyond the core course outcomes through the Challenge Track.  If you choose not to participate, or do not complete the track requirements, your final grade will be capped at a maximum of A-.

Here are the rules of engagement:

### 1. The 85% Gateway

You must maintain an 85% or higher on the regular materials (labs, quizzes, midterms, and final exam) to have your Challenge Track work considered. It makes no sense to attempt advanced workflows if you haven't mastered the core concepts first.

### 2. Process Over Completion

You do not need to hyper-focus on a specific number of completed challenges. Your grade is not a checklist. Instead, your grade will be determined by the depth of your learning journey. I will look at your GitHub commit histories to see how you solved problems and how you progressed over time.

### 3. The Final Reflection & Sharing


Modern developers increasingly work alongside AI tools, so understanding and explaining your own code has become more important than ever. To successfully complete the Challenge Track, you will be required to do the following:

 * Submit a written learning log or reflection detailing your breakthroughs and struggles.

 * Sit down with me for a brief, casual chat during our final lab session to share a piece of code you are most proud of and explain how you built it.

## Who is this for?

 * **You SHOULD do this if:** You are genuinely curious about software development, want to learn how to use professional tools like Git and VSCode, and want to earn an A or A+.

 * **You SHOULD NOT do this if:** You are already struggling with the core material or do not intend to understand the code you submit. The final conversation will require you to explain your work. 

If you’ve got a solid handle on the basics, I highly encourage you to step up to the challenge!
